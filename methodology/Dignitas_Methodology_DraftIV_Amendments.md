## Amendment 1 — Section IV DE CATEGORIIS
### ISO 26000 Core Subject Alignment

*Insert the following table and paragraph at the end of Section IV, after the five category definitions:*

Each Dignitas category, while constitutionally grounded in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, aligns with the core subjects of ISO 26000:2010 — the principal international guidance standard for social responsibility. This alignment is provided for interoperability: entities, partners, and assessors familiar with ISO 26000 can locate each Dignitas category within that framework, and documentation prepared for ISO 26000-based processes (including EcoVadis assessments and Lucie 26000 certification) can serve directly as voluntary disclosure evidence under Dignitas.

| Dignitas Category | UDHR Articles | ISO 26000 Core Subjects |
|---|---|---|
| **Democracy** | 19, 21, 26, 27 | 6.2 Organizational governance · 6.6 Fair operating practices (responsible political involvement) · 6.8 Community involvement and development (education and culture) |
| **Labor** | 20, 23 | 6.4 Labour practices · 6.3 Human rights (fundamental principles and rights at work) |
| **Environment** | 25 | 6.5 The environment |
| **Rights** | 12, 17, 29, 18 | 6.3 Human rights (due diligence, discrimination, civil and political rights) · 6.7 Consumer issues (data protection and privacy) |
| **Finance** | 17 | 6.6 Fair operating practices (anti-corruption, fair competition) · 6.7 Consumer issues (fair marketing, protecting consumers) |

ISO 26000 is a guidance standard and is not certifiable; no entity gains or loses Dignitas points by reference to ISO 26000 itself. The mapping is interpretive.

*Insert the following paragraph after the Labor table:*

The ILO references above correspond to the ILO's fundamental conventions wherever applicable (C87, C98, C29, C105, C138, C182, C155, C187 — eight of the ten fundamental conventions; C100 and C111 on equal remuneration and non-discrimination are covered under the Rights category's non-discrimination indicator). A documented violation of a fundamental ILO convention, as established by ILO supervisory bodies, national labor inspectorates, or courts, constitutes a confirmed signal at the stated severity. This mapping establishes interoperability with SA8000, which is built on the same convention base: an SA8000 audit finding maps directly onto the corresponding Dignitas Labor indicator.

## Amendment 3 — Section VI DE DIVULGATIONE
### New Sub-Section: De Certificationibus Externis — Third-Party Certification Recognition

*Insert as a new sub-section at the end of Section VI, after the existing voluntary disclosure table:*

Entities holding current, verifiable certifications from established external frameworks receive recognition bonuses in the relevant categories. This reflects two facts: first, that undergoing independent external assessment is itself an act of voluntary accountability; second, that the evidence trail generated by these certifications materially improves the data available to the assessment layer.

**Three principles govern this recognition:**

1. **Recognition, never requirement.** Consistent with the founding principle of this section, no third-party certification is required for any Dignitas score or Civitas status. The absence of certification is never penalized and contributes no negative signal. Dignitas remains accessible to entities that cannot afford legacy certification processes.

2. **Currency and verifiability.** A certification qualifies only while it is current and verifiable in the certifier's public registry (B Lab directory, SAAS certified facilities list, EcoVadis public scorecard where shared, UN Global Compact participant database). A lapsed, suspended, or revoked certification removes the bonus at the next recalculation. Revocation for cause is itself recorded as a Medium severity signal in the affected category.

3. **Subordination to observed conduct.** A certification bonus is suspended immediately when a High or Critical severity signal is confirmed in the same category. A certificate cannot shield observed misconduct — conduct outranks credentials. Certification bonuses, like disclosure bonuses, cannot override the floor score of 25 following a Critical violation.

**Recognition table:**

| Certification | Requirement | Category Bonus | Verification Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B Corp (B Lab) | Current certification in good standing | +2 in each of the five categories | B Lab public directory |
| SA8000 (Social Accountability International) | Current certificate from SAAS-accredited body | Labor +5 | SAAS certified facilities list |
| EcoVadis Gold or Platinum | Current medal, scorecard shared publicly or with Gladius | Environment +3 · Labor +3 | EcoVadis scorecard |
| UN Global Compact | Active participant with current Communication on Progress filed | +1 in each of the five categories | UN Global Compact participant database |

The maximum total bonus per category — voluntary disclosures and certification recognition combined — remains **+10 points**, as established in Draft III. Where an entity qualifies for bonuses exceeding the cap, the cap applies.

Certifications not listed may be proposed for recognition through the Comitia governance process. The Council reviews the recognition table annually alongside the general methodology review (Section X), and may suspend recognition of any framework whose own integrity is credibly compromised.

> *A certificate is testimony, not proof. Dignitas hears the testimony — and keeps watching.*
